How it works

Five minutes a day.
A book for a lifetime.

One

A gentle start.

They pick what they love, put their picture on the front of their book and choose when they'd like their question. Daily reminders start enabled; they can switch them off during setup or later at the top of Settings, where they can also change the reminder time. Every step is spoken aloud.

Two

One gentle invitation.

A question or a photo, with room to talk. On supported iPhones, new questions can grow from memories already shared. Talk can quote earlier words when you ask for a reminder, and gives quiet speech more time before replying. Choose another whenever you like; games and music wait in More.

Three

They just talk.

Their words appear as they speak. They can pause as long as they like; the app waits. One button when they're done.

Four

Their book grows.

Every story becomes a page, by month, with their voice a tap away. Old photos get their stories told, too. Through the years shows whole photos with gentle fades.

Questions

Good to know

Does it need the internet?

The core book, recordings and recorded questions work on the iPhone. Optional online voice, Alexa, music services and nearby-place lookups need a connection. Each connection has its own controls.

Who can hear the stories?

The book is kept on the phone. A story is shared only when its owner chooses to share or export it. Optional online voice and Alexa may process conversation text with separate consent; they do not receive the photo library, photos, videos or existing book. See our privacy policy for details, backups and TestFlight version notes.

Can we make a memory together?

From build 43, turn on Shared memories in My Book or Settings. Take or choose a photo, add shared words and send the memory file through the iPhone share sheet. The recipient reviews it and chooses whether to add a page, with their own perspective. This is optional and off by default; it does not match contacts or synchronize books automatically. Both phones need the supporting version.

What if they'd rather type?

Every question can be answered in writing, too.
